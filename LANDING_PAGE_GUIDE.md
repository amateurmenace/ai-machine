# Landing Page Guide

The landing page introduces the Civic AI Engine to someone who has never heard
of it, then to someone deciding whether their station or town should host one,
then to someone who will run it. `LANDING_PAGE_STRUCTURE.md` lists the sections
and the files.

## Key messages

**AI as civic infrastructure.** Public access television made local government
watchable. The Civic AI Engine makes it askable. A public utility that lives at
public access stations, is free for all, and follows rules written by the
community, not Big Tech.

**Two things at once.** A fully functional alternative to the frontier models,
built on the latest open models, and a deeply local one, holding the meetings,
documents and decisions of a single town that Big Tech's models are not indexing.

**What makes it different.**
- A fraction of a data center: one desktop drawing 100–300 W, solar where the
  station has it.
- Residents write the rules: a community constitution, enforced in the model.
- It never leaves the community: open models on one desktop, no cloud.
- Anyone can build on it: a free API for community apps, vibe-coded or not.

**The vision.** Cable companies pay for community media; AI companies should pay
for community AI. Legislation that requires AI companies to fund community AI at
public access stations, the way cable franchise fees have funded community media
for forty years.

**How it works.** Create, collect, learn, fine-tune, engage.

**How it is built.** One node, four layers: power, compute, corpus, private
lane. Runs on a Mac Studio. Accessible by default. Governed, not moderated. Open
all the way down.

## Things the page does not do

- It does not name partner stations, funders or grant programs. The vision is
  told in general terms: stations, a hub, a network, lawmakers.
- It does not quote dollar figures from the brief's partner offer.
- It does not call an API. The demo is a simulation with sample data and says
  so. The live-assistant section only appears when the build is served by the
  API on its own machine.
- It does not present the sample town, sources or answers as real. The default
  town, Riverbend, MA, is fictional.

## Tone

Plain words first. Short sentences. Concrete nouns (a shelf, a wired port, the
clerk's minutes) over abstractions. Technical sections are as precise as the
system guide, but each one starts with the reason it matters to a resident.
